Cyber Insurance Readiness Check for Small Businesses

Your broker sent a cyber insurance questionnaire and you are not sure you can honestly check every box. That is the right worry. Carriers now ask whether controls are enforced, not just whether you bought a tool, and your answers can become conditions of the policy.

The Cyber Insurance Readiness Check is a remote review of your real environment, done before you submit the application or renew. You get a plain list of what passes, what does not, and what to fix first.

Why this matters

Cyber insurance applications are getting harder to pass, and the numbers show why.

  • According to Delinea's 2023 State of Cyber Insurance report, 28% of small companies that applied for cyber insurance were denied coverage, compared with 8% of large companies.

What do cyber insurance underwriters ask small businesses?

  • Multi factor authentication: email, remote access, admin accounts, backup consoles, and any exceptions. Partial coverage is usually treated as no.
  • Backups: offline or separated from your network, and tested for restore.
  • Endpoint detection and response on every device.
  • Patching, end of life systems, and security awareness training records.
  • A written incident response plan and wire transfer verification steps.

Requirements differ by carrier and coverage amount, so your broker's questionnaire is the final word. We work from it.

How it works

  1. Call or send us the questionnaire, ideally 60 to 90 days before renewal.
  2. We review your Microsoft 365 or Google settings, devices, backups and policies remotely.
  3. You get a written findings list with each answer marked ready, fixable, or not true yet.
  4. We help you close gaps and keep the evidence your broker or carrier may ask for.

FAQ

Frequently asked questions

How do I know if my business will pass a cyber insurance application?

Compare the questionnaire to your real settings, not your assumptions. Carriers look for enforced MFA, tested backups kept offline, endpoint protection, staff training, patching and a written incident response plan. A readiness check tests each item in your environment and tells you which answers you can honestly give.

Does cyber insurance require MFA everywhere?

Carriers ask exactly where it is enforced, so expect questions about email, remote access and admin accounts. Partial rollout, such as MFA for some staff only, often counts as no. Read the wording on your own application, because requirements differ by carrier and coverage amount.

What happens if I answer the application wrong?

Your answers can become conditions of the policy. If you say MFA covers all remote access and an unprotected entry point is used in an attack, the carrier may dispute the claim or try to cancel the policy. Check only boxes you can prove, and have someone review them first.

How much does cyber insurance cost for a small business?

Hiscox says small business cyber insurance starts at about $30 a month, though the price depends on your industry, coverage limit and deductible. Your broker gives the real quote. The premium is usually small next to the cost of a denied application or a disputed claim.

Why was my cyber insurance application denied?

The most common reason is missing security basics. In Delinea's 2023 survey, 28% of small companies that applied were denied, and lack of security protocols was the top reason at 40%. Ask your broker which question failed, fix that control, then reapply with evidence.

No. Carriers make the underwriting decision and set the price. We find and help fix the control gaps that commonly cause declined applications, so your answers are accurate and your evidence is organized. Anyone promising approval or a specific premium before the carrier reviews you is guessing.
